## Design Specificity Verdict

**Content-specific, composition-generic.** Cinzel campaign naming, HP/AC summaries, party terminology, and role-aware controls make the content recognizably D&D. The composition is still an interchangeable administration dashboard: toolbar, muted status rail, repeated lists, paired forms, and indigo calls to action.

The page misses the product's "lamp-lit table" goal because its dominant experience is bureaucracy rather than campaign readiness. It also contradicts the approved V2 architecture: active play belongs on Character Sheet and DM Screen, while Campaign Overview should orient, summarize, and configure.

**Deterministic scan:** the static CLI scan returned zero findings. The injected browser detector reported 58 hits across 54 elements: 52 `undersized-ui-text`, 5 `tiny-text`, and 1 `overused-font`. The Inter finding is a false positive because Inter is the intentional product typeface. The text-size findings were measured on authenticated DOM that was hidden by the unauthenticated error state, so the live error screenshot could not visually confirm every case; the CSS and markup indicate they remain relevant once populated.

**Browser evidence:** both assessments used fresh isolated tabs. The API was unavailable in the local static environment, so authentic populated data could not load. Assessment A populated source-faithful representative DM/player states at 1440 px and 390 px; mobile had no horizontal overflow but the DM page exceeded four viewports. Assessment B successfully injected the detector in an automated tab, but no durable user-visible overlay remains because the critique-only servers were stopped.

## Overall Impression

The implementation is careful, accessible, and operationally trustworthy, but the page has no single job. Its strongest opportunity is not prettier cards: remove active-play work from this route and make the first viewport answer, "Is this campaign ready, and what needs my attention?"

## What's Working

1. **Accessible foundations are unusually strong.** Landmarks, skip navigation, explicit labels, fieldsets, live regions, focus treatments, large controls, and reduced-motion handling establish a sound base.
2. **Network and failure behavior protects trust.** Stale-data warnings and "your data was not changed" language are much more reassuring than generic error banners.
3. **Role-aware rendering and responsive stacking are coherent.** Player and DM affordances are separated, and mobile reflows without horizontal overflow.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Campaign Overview has conflicting purposes

**Why it matters:** players and DMs cannot tell whether the page is for opening play, resolving requests, manipulating characters, administering membership, or configuring rules. Every added capability makes the route worse.

**Fix:** move effect creation/approval to Character Sheet, DM effects/grants to DM Screen and contextual sheet actions, and inventory transfers to sheet inventory. Keep Campaign Overview focused on readiness, roster, attention items, activity, and setup.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] The first viewport has no decisive hierarchy

**Why it matters:** campaign identity, account identity, duplicate party summaries, connection state, shortcuts, and actions compete before the user knows what needs attention.

**Fix:** present one role-specific primary action (`Open character` or `Open DM workspace`), a compact readiness/status line, and an attention queue. Demote account controls and administration to the global shell or a secondary settings surface.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] Consequential actions lack an impact preview and recovery model

**Why it matters:** effects, grants, transfers, and policy publication can change another person's character or campaign rules. Default-selected targets and terse success states increase the cost of hurried mistakes.

**Fix:** where these actions remain during migration, show actor, target, current value, resulting value, scope, and reversibility before commit. Add explicit undo/revoke where semantics permit; otherwise state permanence and give a recovery action.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Two roster concepts force users to model implementation details

**Why it matters:** "My characters," "Party roster," local originals, cloud copies, and campaign members overlap without a clear primary entity. First-time players cannot confidently predict which object opens or changes.

**Fix:** use one party roster with ownership/access badges and a clear attach/copy state. Put local-to-campaign migration in a focused onboarding flow rather than alongside normal roster use.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Mobile preserves the form wall instead of the urgent task

**Why it matters:** desktop jump shortcuts disappear while pending requests and role-primary actions can be pushed several viewports down. The detector also found widespread sub-14px UI text, weakening glanceability during play.

**Fix:** keep the role-primary action and attention count near the top, make urgent requests reachable through a compact sticky/subnav affordance, and raise UI text to the established readable token floor. Administration remains collapsed.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Target Shape and Component Map

This is the recommended Campaign Overview shape after mid-play responsibilities move:

```text
Global shell
└─ Active campaign switcher · account · local mode

Campaign overview
├─ Campaign header
│  ├─ name · role · connection/capability state
│  └─ one role-primary CTA
├─ Attention queue
│  └─ pending approvals, policy warnings, invitations, recoverable failures
├─ Party roster
│  └─ one character model with owner/access/privacy/compliance badges
├─ Session snapshot
│  └─ party readiness, shared inventory summary, next-session information
├─ Recent activity
│  └─ character-first readable events with filtering/pagination
└─ Setup and administration
   ├─ people and invitations
   ├─ rules and source policy
   ├─ campaign homebrew
   └─ lifecycle and export
```

The page should not contain generic effect, transfer, XP, or item-grant forms in its final state. During migration, those controls should live behind a clearly labeled legacy disclosure rather than define the main composition.

## Cognitive Load

**7/8 checklist failures — high load.** Only visual grouping passes. The page fails single focus, chunking to four or fewer items, clear hierarchy, one decision at a time, minimal choices, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure.

Overloaded decision points include:

- six effect types;
- nine spell-slot levels;
- transfer source, recipient, item, quantity, and five currencies at once;
- six rules controls;
- more than four competing initial paths;
- four simultaneous DM "At the table" workflows.

## Emotional Journey

The campaign name, role, active badge, and connection state create a reassuring entrance. Confidence then collapses into duplicate rosters and a long wall of forms. High-stakes actions lack a before/after preview, while successful outcomes are terse. The page ends on administration disclosures, so the remembered ending is configuration work rather than "the party is ready."

## Persona Red Flags

**First-time player:** cannot distinguish local originals, cloud copies, owned characters, and party members; empty states explain absence but rarely teach the next correct step.

**In-session player under time pressure:** must leave the sheet, scroll, interpret default-selected targets, and navigate forms for effects or transfers. Mobile hides shortcuts rather than prioritizing urgent approvals.

**DM preparing or running a session:** setup health is reduced to small rail metadata while operational forms dominate. Awards are character-by-character rather than previewable session/batch actions, and there is no concise readiness view.

## Minor Observations

- Legacy blue site chrome and the newer indigo Hub surface feel stitched together.
- Tiny uppercase metadata and distant timestamps slow scanning.
- Signed-in account identity occupies prime campaign-toolbar space without helping the current task.
- Empty states are clear but should recommend the next valid action.
- Detector emphasis on Inter is not actionable; the font is intentional and should remain.

## Questions to Consider

1. If active play belongs on Character Sheet and DM Screen, should this route answer only, "Is this campaign ready?"
2. Would anyone miss "At the table" if it disappeared after equivalent sheet/DM interactions shipped?
3. Is the primary roster entity a person, a character, or a cloud copy?
4. Should DM awards become a previewable end-of-session batch rather than immediate individual mutations?

## Recommended Actions

1. **`/impeccable shape`** — define the role-based Campaign Overview information architecture and migrate mid-play responsibilities to Character Sheet/DM Screen.
2. **`/impeccable distill`** — reduce the first viewport to campaign readiness, one primary action, attention, and party state.
3. **`/impeccable clarify`** — unify roster/copy terminology and replace implementation language with player/DM language.
4. **`/impeccable harden`** — add preview, undo/recovery, and complete loading/empty/error/offline behavior to consequential actions.
5. **`/impeccable adapt`** — design mobile priority, persistent attention access, and readable UI text.
6. **`/impeccable polish`** — perform the bounded final quality pass after the responsibilities and hierarchy are stable.
